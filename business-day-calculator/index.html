Business day calculator

Count the business days between two dates, or find the date N business days from a start date, skipping weekends and the holidays of a chosen calendar: US federal holidays, US banks (Federal Reserve), NYSE, UK (England and Wales) bank holidays or euro T2. It runs entirely in your browser, with no tracking and no network calls. Dates from 2026 to 2028 are supported, because those are the years with verified holiday lists.


Result: Needs JavaScript. See the worked examples below.

How days are counted

  • Business day: Monday to Friday that is not on the chosen calendar's holiday list.
  • Between, start date included (default): both the start and the end date are counted if they are business days, the same as Excel's NETWORKDAYS(start, end, holidays). Monday to Friday of one week is 5. If the end is before the start the result is negative.
  • Between, start date not included: counts the days after the start up to and including the end, so the same start and end date gives 0 and Monday to Friday of one week is 4.
  • Add N: the start date is not counted, the same as Excel's WORKDAY(start, N, holidays): 1 business day after a Friday is the next Monday, and from a weekend or holiday the count starts from the next business day. N = 0 returns the start date unchanged, and a negative N goes back.

Calendars

US federal uses observed dates (a Saturday holiday is observed on Friday, a Sunday holiday on Monday). Federal Reserve, NYSE, England and Wales and euro T2 use their own closing days. Weekday holidays only, 2026-2028. Check the lists on the Excel holiday lists page, which also has free xlsx and CSV downloads, or see business days between two dates in SQL for PostgreSQL and DuckDB. In code: the free open-source us-federal-calendar (Python, US federal) and world-public-holidays (public holiday data).

Holidays come from the open-source holidays package and were checked against QuantLib. Future dates follow current holiday rules; unscheduled closures cannot be predicted. Early closes are not holidays here. Check official sources before payroll, legal or settlement use.
